The science

Four devices. One fair score.

No two wearables read recovery the same way. The Sensor Ontology is the layer that makes them comparable — here's the actual pipeline behind your number.

Step 1 · Raw signals

Every wearable, raw

Each device exports a different number from different math — and none of them line up.

Oura RMSSDApple SDNNGarmin Body BatteryWhoop recovery %
Step 2 · Sensor Ontology

Mapped to one physiology

Each raw metric is matched to the same underlying signals — so a heartbeat is a heartbeat, whatever read it.

HRVResting HRSleep depthStrain
Step 3 · Personal baseline

Measured against you

Your trailing 30 days set your normal. Today is scored against your own baseline — not a population average.

30-day baselinePercentile mapping
Step 4 · Oriya Score
0–100

Mapped to one shared scale. That's why an Oura 85 and a Whoop 85 are finally the same 85 — and why you can compete fairly with anyone.

The network sharpens itself. Every new device type teaches the Sensor Ontology another dialect of recovery — so normalization gets more accurate as more athletes join. The science compounds.
