You can't convert an Oura score to a Whoop score.

The problem

A Whoop 83 isn't an Oura 83.

Comparing scores from two different wearables directly is like comparing Celsius to Fahrenheit — same digit, completely different meaning. The numbers aren't built the same way: an independent review of 14 composite scores across 10 brands found they use undisclosed, inconsistently weighted formulas and are rarely validated. Even the raw inputs diverge — measuring the same heartbeats, HRV agreement ranged from near-perfect on one device to poor on another. Put these on a shared leaderboard untouched and the most generous algorithm wins, not the best recovery.

That rough −8 is only a day-one placeholder. There's no honest formula to convert one brand into another — so the real fix, below, is to rank each of you against your own baseline.

The honest answer

So we don't guess a conversion — we compare you to yourself.

There's no public constant that turns an Oura score into a Whoop score: the brands don't publish their weights, and anyone who claims to have the formula is guessing. So Oriya doesn't convert between brands. It reads your own recent history and finds your personal floor, par and ceiling — then today's score becomes a percentile inside your distribution, and that percentile maps onto the shared 0–100 index.

your score today → percentile in your own 90 days → shared 0–100
your device's quirks sit in both the reading and the baseline — so they cancel
on the shared scale, Par 71 is where a typical morning lands — for everyone. Boards rank your gap to par, so “over par” means beating your own usual, whoever you are and whatever you wear.
What it buys you

A Whoop wearer and an Oura wearer on the same board — where rank means “who's closest to their own best,” not “whose algorithm is more generous.”

What it costs

You need about 14 days of history before your number is honest — the percentile reads your last ~90 days, and until roughly day 14 there isn't enough of it. We show a provisional score before then — and we label it provisional.

Before we know your baseline

On day one there's no history to rank against — so a single screenshot can't be a real baseline percentile yet. To get you on the board immediately, we apply a rough, per-device correction and mark it clearly. It's a starting estimate, not the science.

End to end

What happens to your score.

01

Read the raw score

Pulled straight from your screenshot and OCR-verified against the device's own metric label, or read directly through a read-only connection.

02

Rank it against your own baseline

With ~14 days of history, your score becomes a percentile inside your own floor-to-ceiling range. On a first screenshot, a provisional per-device estimate stands in — clearly labelled until your baseline exists.

03

Weight the full signal

Connected directly, Oriya's own weighting blends sleep, recovery (HRV + resting HR) and activity — the same contributors the research finds in every brand's score — instead of trusting one opaque composite.

04

Post to the board

Your normalized number joins the squad leaderboard — ranked against your own baseline, never a raw public score. Par 71 marks a typical morning on the shared scale; rank is the gap you hold above your own par.

How you can check our work

The one dataset that would let us publish real constants.

The dual-wearer calibration

The only honest way to publish a cross-device conversion is data from people wearing two devices at once — same body, two numbers, every morning. We're recruiting the first dual-wearers now. At 500, we publish the empirical mapping: the actual factors, their confidence intervals, and the sample size behind each one — and you get to tell us we're wrong.

Where it comes from

Grounded in the research.

The research doesn't hand us a conversion formula — it explains why one can't exist. That's the whole reason we compare you to your own baseline instead of converting between brands.

1 · You can't derive a conversion from these scores. Doherty C, Baldwin M, Lambe R, Burke D, Altini M. "Readiness, recovery, and strain: an evaluation of composite health scores in consumer wearables." Translational Exercise Biomedicine, 2025;2(2):128–144.14 scores across 10 brands: the common inputs are HRV (86%) and resting HR (79%), but the weightings and formulas are undisclosed and the scores are rarely validated against a reference. There is no published constant to look up — so we don't pretend to have one.

2 · Devices don't even agree on the raw signal. Miller DJ, Sargent C, Roach GD. "A Validation of Six Wearable Devices for Estimating Sleep, Heart Rate and Heart Rate Variability in Healthy Adults." Sensors, 2022;22(16):6317.Measuring the same heartbeats, HRV agreement ran from excellent (ICC 0.99) to poor (ICC 0.24), and sleep-stage accuracy spanned 50–65%. When devices disagree on the underlying physiology, converting one brand's composite into another's only compounds the error — one more reason to rank you against yourself.

3 · HRV metrics aren't interchangeable. Shaffer F, Ginsberg JP. "An Overview of Heart Rate Variability Metrics and Norms." Frontiers in Public Health, 2017;5:258.RMSSD and SDNN are distinct measures (healthy SDNN ≈ 50 ms) — a value on one scale can't be read off as the other.

4 · Against orthosomnia. Baron KG, Abbott S, Jao N, Manalo N, Mullen R. "Orthosomnia: Are Some Patients Taking the Quantified Self Too Far?" Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2017;13(2):351–354.Fixating on a tracker number can worsen the very thing it measures — so rank is against your own baseline, and a red morning is never a public failure.

Honest about the limits. The research above is candid that these scores are proprietary, inconsistently weighted, and seldom validated — which is exactly why we don't convert between them. Comparing you to your own baseline is the fair mechanism; the single-screenshot factors are a provisional stand-in, labelled as such, until your history exists. The Oriya Index is built to be directionally useful for fair comparison, not diagnostically precise — a social context layer, not a medical device, and no substitute for clinical assessment. And we won't weaponize it: rank is measured against your own baseline, so a hard night never becomes a public failure.
Your data

Built private by default.

The whole point is to share a number with people you choose — which only works if you stay in control of everything underneath it.

Read-only access

When you connect a device, Oriya requests read-only scopes. We never get your account password, and we can't write or change anything in your wearable account.

Sharing is off by default

Granular toggles control exactly what each squad or challenge sees. Nothing is shared until you turn it on, and you can export or delete your data at any time.

Teams see aggregate only

For workplace challenges, an admin only ever sees aggregate stats — and only when at least five people are participating, so no individual is ever singled out.

Never sold, never advertised

Your recovery data is not a product. We don't sell it, we don't run ads against it, and we design data minimization in from the start.
