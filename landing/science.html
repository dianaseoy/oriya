The Science · for Season 1 athletes

One fair number, across every wearable.

Oura reads Readiness. Whoop reads Recovery. Garmin rolls a Body Battery. Each is built on a different sensor, a different model, and a different validation standard — so the raw numbers were never comparable. Here's exactly how we make them comparable.

Grounded in peer-reviewed research · 12 devices supported
The problem

A Whoop 83 isn't an Oura 83.

Comparing scores from two different wearables directly is like comparing Celsius to Fahrenheit — same digit, completely different meaning. The numbers aren't built the same way: an independent review of 14 composite scores across 10 brands found they use undisclosed, inconsistently weighted formulas and are rarely validated. Even the raw inputs diverge — measuring the same heartbeats, HRV agreement ranged from near-perfect on one device to poor on another. Put these on a shared leaderboard untouched and the most generous algorithm wins, not the best recovery.

The model

A calibrated correction, per device.

Every device's native score is converted to the Oriya Standard with a simple, transparent linear correction — a factor and an offset tuned for that specific sensor. Devices with weaker independent validation get a larger correction. The result is clamped to a 1–99 scale.

Oriya = round( native × factor + offset )

Where it comes from

Grounded in the research.

The correction factors are informed by independent evaluations of how consumer wearables' composite recovery scores relate to each other and to the underlying physiology.

1 · Composite scores aren't comparable. Doherty C, Baldwin M, Lambe R, Burke D, Altini M. "Readiness, recovery, and strain: an evaluation of composite health scores in consumer wearables." Translational Exercise Biomedicine, 2025;2(2):128–144.14 scores across 10 brands. HRV (86%), resting HR (79%), activity and sleep are the common inputs — but weightings and formulas are undisclosed, and the scores are rarely validated against a reference.

2 · Devices measure the body differently. Miller DJ, Sargent C, Roach GD. "A Validation of Six Wearable Devices for Estimating Sleep, Heart Rate and Heart Rate Variability in Healthy Adults." Sensors, 2022;22(16):6317.Measuring the same heartbeats, HRV agreement ran from excellent (ICC 0.99, ±4.7 ms on Whoop) to poor (ICC 0.24, ±33.1 ms on Garmin); sleep-stage accuracy spanned 50–65%.

3 · HRV metrics aren't interchangeable. Shaffer F, Ginsberg JP. "An Overview of Heart Rate Variability Metrics and Norms." Frontiers in Public Health, 2017;5:258.RMSSD and SDNN are distinct measures (healthy SDNN ≈ 50 ms) — a value on one scale can't be read off as the other.

4 · Privacy by design. U.S. Federal Trade Commission, In re Flo Health, Inc., Docket No. C-4747 (final order, June 22, 2021).A health app that shared data without clear consent drew FTC enforcement — which is why Oriya defaults to affirmative opt-in and read-only access.

5 · Against orthosomnia. Baron KG, Abbott S, Jao N, Manalo N, Mullen R. "Orthosomnia: Are Some Patients Taking the Quantified Self Too Far?" Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2017;13(2):351–354.Fixating on a tracker number can worsen the very thing it measures — so rank is against your own baseline, and a red morning is never a public failure.

Honest about the limits. The research above is candid that these scores are proprietary, inconsistently weighted, and seldom validated — which is exactly why comparing them raw fails, and why we publish our correction openly instead of hiding it. The Oriya Standard is built to be directionally useful for fair comparison, not diagnostically precise. It is a social context layer, not a medical device, and is no substitute for clinical assessment. And we won't weaponize it: rank is measured against your own baseline, so a hard night never becomes a public failure.
Your data

Built private by default.

The whole point is to share a number with people you choose — which only works if you stay in control of everything underneath it.

Read-only access

When you connect a device, Oriya requests read-only scopes. We never get your account password, and we can't write or change anything in your wearable account.

Sharing is off by default

Granular toggles control exactly what each squad or challenge sees. Nothing is shared until you turn it on, and you can export or delete your data at any time.

Teams see aggregate only

For workplace challenges, an admin only ever sees aggregate stats — and only when at least five people are participating, so no individual is ever singled out.

Never sold, never advertised

Your recovery data is not a product. We don't sell it, we don't run ads against it, and we design data minimization in from the start.
