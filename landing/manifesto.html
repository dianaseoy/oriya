The Oriya Manifesto · v1 · founder-written

A decade of your baseline, deleted because you changed a wristband.

Your body is quietly becoming the most-measured surface in your life — the ring, the watch, the strap, the mattress, the glucose patch, and whatever ships next quarter. Each one keeps a private log about you, in its own dialect, on its own server, and hands back a verdict instead of the signal. That log is the most valuable record you'll ever generate — and today, none of it is yours. Here's why that happens, where it's heading as health leaves the hospital, and the bet we're making on who holds the keys.

01 · Why nobody fixes this

They could put every device on one scale tomorrow. Agreeing would cost them you.

An Oura 62 is not a Whoop 62. The scores are mathematically incompatible — different algorithms weighting HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory-rate variance and sleep staging in ways none of them will show you. That isn't a technical accident anyone's racing to fix. It's the moat.

As long as your history is trapped in one company's private dialect, leaving means starting over at zero — and that friction is exactly what keeps you paying. Interoperability would hand you the exit. So your lifetime continuity isn't a feature they forgot to build; it's collateral they're structurally paid to withhold. The reset isn't a bug — it's the business model.

Illustrative — one night, four devices, four verdicts. Today it's four; the count only climbs — rings, patches, mattresses, glucose sensors all lining up behind them. Apple ships the raw signals but no native readiness score at all. Zero portability — by design, not by accident.

The recovery score used to be a private verdict you read alone at dawn. It's becoming a shared, longitudinal thing — and status is inverting with it.

02 · From solo anxiety to shared record

Readiness stops being a number you stare at alone.

For twenty years, the flex was depletion — how wrecked you could prove you were, how little you slept during the crunch. That signal is inverting. The new flex is preparedness: the resting heart rate you held flat through a launch window, the HRV you defended across a three-day red-team.

A readiness number is far more useful compared than confessed — but how you compare it decides whether the game is honest. Rank raw recovery scores on a public board and you haven't built a competition; you've built a genetics leaderboard — where the youngest body on the most generous wearable wins a race nobody actually ran. The fair version normalizes every device onto one scale, then reads each person against their own baseline — so the flex is the recovery you defended under load, not the resting heart rate you were born with. That's the only version worth making social: status through provable recovery, never a shaming number — and never a number you were simply lucky to wake up with.

03 · The bet

One record. Portable. Yours.

Here's what Oriya is betting on — stated plainly as a bet, not a shipped feature: a Body Passport. A single, longitudinal record of your recovery, normalized across every device onto one comparable index, that you carry from hardware to hardware for the length of your life.

Not a dozen slices scattered across a dozen vaults, each in its own dialect, renegotiated every morning. One continuity — it travels from whatever you wear today to whatever ships in 2031. And the timing is the whole point: health is drifting away from the hospital, where you're a stranger until you're sick and a snapshot once you are, toward something continuous, individually owned, and predictive — a model that reads your body and gets ahead of the diagnosis instead of confirming it. When that model arrives, it should read a record you govern, on terms you set — an asset in your name, not inventory in someone else's, and not a signal an LLM on a vendor's server quietly prices around you.

Pillar 01

Portable

Switch devices without amnesia. Your baseline travels; it doesn't reset to zero because you changed a strap. Continuity is the whole point.

Pillar 02

Longitudinal

A lifetime record, not a seven-day rolling window. Decades of your autonomic baseline in one continuous line — the only frame in which prevention makes sense.

Pillar 03

Preventive

Pointed at the slow drift, not the acute crisis. Catch the creep before it's a diagnosis. Directionally useful, not diagnostically precise — and we'll always say which.

Pillar 04

Sovereign

You own it. Aggregate-only, never sold. The record is a passport in your name — not a data product someone quietly resells around you.

Where we actually are

Read this as intent, not a press release.

This is a manifesto — the direction we're pointed, written by the person building it. So here's the honest ledger, no spin:

  • The portable, lifetime Body Passport is the bet — the vision we're building toward. It is not a finished, shipped product, and we won't pretend otherwise.
  • What's real today: recovery normalization across devices, and opt-in boards and stakes among people who choose to compare. Early, founder-run, private beta.
  • Your data is aggregate-only and never sold. Team views need a minimum group size; individual sharing is off by default.
  • No user counts and no partner logos on this page — because we're not going to invent them. There are no signed partners yet. You'd be early.

The long version isn't on this page. Oriya's founder set out the full argument in a separate thesis — The Body Has an API Now. Who Holds the Keys? — that the company that matters won't own the sensor, but the signal every device writes about you. This manifesto is the compressed cut.
