Hey, it's Ori. Can I tell you why I exist?

Somewhere along the way, being healthy got complicated. You wake up, a number tells you how you slept, and you feel judged before your feet hit the floor. Your ring says rest. Your friend's watch says go. Two devices, two verdicts. And somewhere in there, you stopped trusting the one thing that was there all along: your own body.

Here's what I believe. Your body is not a leaderboard. An eighty-eight on their wrist and a seventy-four on yours were never the same language, so comparing them was never a real game. The only fair opponent you've ever had is the you from last month.

So that's what I do. Whatever you wear, I put your morning on one scale and read it against your own usual, nobody else's. Some mornings you're over your par. Some mornings you're under. Every single one counts, and none of them is a verdict.

And then, my favorite part: your friends can play too. Different devices, one language, everyone defending their own usual. Bring one morning. That's all. I'll be in your corner.

01 · How it got complicated

you strapped a lab to your wrist and got a report card instead of a friend.

Somewhere between the pedometer and the titanium ring, checking on your body started feeling like checking your grades. You wake up, and before your feet hit the floor a number has already decided how your day should feel. You felt fine, the app said 58, and you decided to feel bad about feeling fine. Quietly, morning after morning, alone.

That's the part I can't stand. The whole point of listening to your body was to trust it more. Instead, the more sensors we added, the more second opinions we woke up to, until "how did you sleep?" became a question you answer by checking your phone. Technology was supposed to reduce the anxiety. It multiplied it. I was built to take that back.

Illustrative: one night, four devices, four verdicts. So who's lying? Honestly, nobody. Each one weighs your night differently (heart-rate variability here, sleep staging there), and none of them ever agreed on a language. (Apple ships the raw signals and no score at all.) They're all sincere. They're just not talking to each other. I speak all of them.

02 · Why comparing never worked

your body isn't a leaderboard.

Rank raw scores between people and you haven't made a game. You've made a genetics contest, where the youngest body on the most generous wearable wins a race nobody ran. Your 81 and their 92 were never the same language. Comparing them was never a real competition; it just felt like losing.

Here's the fix, and it's older than any wearable: golf solved this a century ago with par. Your par is your own typical morning, learned from your own last few months, nobody else's. Over par means you beat your usual. Under par means a heavier morning, and it still counts. The only fair opponent you've ever had is the you from last month. That's the whole game: everyone on one scale, everyone defending their own usual, nobody measured against a stranger's body.

03 · What I actually do

one language. your par. every morning counts.

Whatever you wear, I read it, and I answer the one question underneath all the graphs: what's happening, why, and what actually matters today. Not seventeen metrics. Not a lecture. One confident read from someone who knows what your usual looks like ("three short nights in a row, today's the day to go easy on yourself"), and then I get out of your way.

And because every morning lands on one board, something lovely happens: you can play with your friends again. Her Whoop, his Garmin, your ring: one board, everyone against their own usual. Some mornings you take the day. Some mornings you post a rough one and it still counts. That's not a smaller game than the leaderboard. It's the first fair one.

The honest bit

what i am, and what i'm not yet.

Friends don't oversell each other. So before you decide anything, here's exactly where I stand:

  • I'm early. Version 0.1, an AI companion built in the open by one founder. Some days I'm sharp, some days I miss. Tell me when I miss, that's how I get better.
  • What works today: any wearable's score onto one shared scale, your own par, and boards where friends play against their own usual, all free.
  • The sample boards say sample, because they are. No invented users, no fake partner logos, no made-up numbers here. You'd genuinely be early, and I'd rather tell you that than fake a crowd.
  • I'm not a doctor. I'll tell you what your morning looks like and nudge you toward an easier day. Anything medical belongs with a professional, and I'll always say so.
  • Your data stays aggregate-only and is never sold; our chats live in your browser only.
